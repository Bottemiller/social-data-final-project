The Global Pulse

Melbourne: the city of… hmm, not sure. As exchange students from the United States, our team was curious about this Australian city. After discovering that our exchange friend, Tracy, was from Melbourne, we couldn't help but want to dig into the "pulse" of the city. Is it more like Copenhagen or Houston, Texas?

To answer this, we analyzed the 2025 hourly trends of pedestrians using the City of Melbourne open data, generated from over 60 sensors placed strategically throughout the Central Business District. By exploring these patterns, we can begin to understand the "why" behind the way this city moves.

Figure 1: Weekday Grind vs. Weekend Flow. This graph compares the average pedestrian counts during the week and on the weekend. Click on a point to discover the exact pedestrian count at that time. Notice the trimodal pattern of the weekday line which represents the morning, lunch and afternoon rush whereas the weekend shows a smooth curve. The weekends allow for the social culture of the city to naturally flow whereas the weekdays follow a busy workweek schedule.

The Daily Grind

During the week, we expect to see a morning rush, a lunch rush, and a late afternoon rush as workers head home. As anticipated, the weekday pedestrian distribution follows a "trimodal" pattern with clear spikes at 8 am, 1 pm, and 5 pm. This rhythm is relatively unsurprising, given the typical 8 to 5 routine followed by most major global cities.

What is surprising, however, is the "reality" of the weekends. While people are forced into a rigid routine during the work week, the city's natural flow emerges on Saturday and Sunday. On these days, the curve is smooth, peaking around 2 pm. This suggests that Melbourne's residents are far more laid back than the weekday construct shows. Another weekend prime time includes a peak at 10 pm; this is significant because it shows how the city has been economically curated for a culture of lively bars and cafes.

The Friday Bridge

While Monday through Thursday are predictable, Friday acts as a bridge to the weekend festivities. Friday's "afternoon rush" is more spread out as workers head home early or walk to post-work plans. Additionally, Friday night is significantly more active compared to other weeknights, signaling the revival of nightlife. In the data, Saturday night looks almost identical to Friday night, proving that Melbourne's “pulse” isn't simply about the sun rising, it’s about the social culture of its people.

The Journal of Transport Geography emphasizes the concept of "spatiotemporal relationships," which is the idea that specific characteristics of an area, like land use, impact pedestrian volume. For example, if you select La Trobe St. (West) in the filter, you see a classic "weekday" street dominated by office space. In contrast, Queen Victoria Market is a "weekend" street; its status as a famous "point of interest" creates a continuous, flowing volume of people that ignores the office schedule.

A City in Motion

While the heatmap allows us to see how Fridays differ from Mondays, the animated map reveals a universal rhythm. Regardless of the day, the pulse follows a familiar cycle: morning peaks at transit hubs, a midday rush at the markets, and a gradual calm at night. According to the Australian National Center for Transport and Mobility Research and Development, the number of vehicles entering the city center has dropped by 14%, and commuting by car has decreased by 25%.

As fewer people rely on cars, more are walking or biking, making pedestrian sensor data the most important tool for modern city planning. As Melbourne designs its walkways for a rapidly growing population, prioritizing walkability based on this sensor data is crucial.

The Verdict

Ultimately, our research into the “pulse” of Melbourne revealed a city that is constantly shifting. The smooth weekend curves and 2 pm peaks feel like the leisure-heavy culture of Copenhagen, whereas the rigid weekday spikes reveal a "Houston" side of the city. This flexibility is Melbourne's greatest strength; a city that can shift from a work-focused financial hub to a lively neighborhood at the change of a clock. Our research revealed one more thing: we definitely know where our next travel spot will be.
